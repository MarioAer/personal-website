## 1. Purpose

A personal website that presents Mario Erazo, a cloud architect, and his work in solution architecture, engineering leadership and AI-assisted engineering. The site is not a CV. It states which problems Mario solves, shows evidence, and makes contact easy.

The same specification is implemented by several AI models, and the visitor can switch between the implementations with a "Built with" selector in the top bar. The specification itself is readable from the site. The mechanic is a demonstration of the third area of expertise (AI-assisted engineering): the same specification, several agents, comparable results.

## 3. Positioning

### 3.1 One-line positioning

Cloud architect. Solution architecture, engineering leadership and AI-assisted engineering for cloud-native commerce platforms.

### 3.2 Areas of expertise

Three areas. Each has a name, a one-sentence outcome, four topics, and two or three evidence items. A topic is a generic name of one to three words with a one-sentence description of the work behind it; variants use the topic names as written. Each evidence item is one sentence of 10 to 30 words that names a common problem and the result the work produces. Evidence is true and generic: it describes recurring work, not a single event, and contains no counts, amounts, percentages, client sectors or tool lists. Variants must present all three areas; order and layout are free.

**Solution architecture and integration**

Outcome: Platforms that stay simple while the number of clients, systems and teams grows.

Topics:

- **Platform design.** Platforms on cloud infrastructure and container orchestration, designed so that client-specific customisation extends the shared product instead of fragmenting it.
- **Integration.** Integration layers across synchronous APIs, messaging and file-based exchange.
- **Domain modelling.** Commerce and marketplace scenarios with multiple sourcing, delivery and fulfilment options.
- **Cost management.** Infrastructure consolidated, and heavy tooling replaced by leaner alternatives.

Evidence:

- Client and partner systems that each speak a different protocol connect to one commerce platform without fragmenting the shared product.
- Cloud bills that grow with every new tool shrink once infrastructure is consolidated and heavy tooling gives way to leaner alternatives.

**Engineering leadership**

Outcome: Technical direction and delivery for distributed teams.

Topics:

- **Development standards.** Technical standards and review processes, set for every team.
- **Platform simplification.** Components and technical standards unified across a platform that has grown service by service.
- **Delivery organisation.** Teams across operations, development and quality assurance led as one organisation, with client requirements, product initiatives and compliance obligations translated into prioritised work packages, and the relationship between engineering and clients managed.
- **Release cadence.** Interfaces and delivery sequencing coordinated with other teams and contractors, and delivery processes aligned across teams.

Evidence:

- Distributed teams that pull in different directions become one delivery organisation with shared standards and clear priorities.
- A platform that has grown service by service becomes simpler to operate once components and technical standards are unified.
- Slow, unpredictable release cycles become short and regular once delivery processes are aligned across teams.

**AI-assisted engineering**

Outcome: Codebases and pipelines that agents can work in safely, and teams that know how to use them.

Topics:

- **Agent-ready codebases.** Standardised repository structure, co-located dependent code, and architecture and agent instruction files at repository and module level.
- **Context engineering.** Shared tooling that gives agents the context of every repository, its relations and the company's rules.
- **Guardrails.** Specifications and review gates for AI-assisted development, introduced to the team as shared practice.
- **Faster delivery.** Repositories and documentation structured so that changes stop stalling on missing context.

Evidence:

- Changes that stall on missing context reach delivery faster once repositories and their documentation are structured for engineers and agents alike.
- Agents that know nothing about a company's systems receive the context of every repository, its relations and the company's rules through shared Claude Code tooling.
- Built this website from one specification that several AI models implement independently, so that their results can be compared directly.

### 3.4 How I work

Seven principles. Each has a generic title of one or two words and a text of one to three sentences, at most 50 words; variants use the titles as written. The text is written in the first person. Variants present the principles as a list or as short paragraphs.

1. **Context first.** I gather the business and technical context first, investigate the options and weigh their trade-offs. I write an RFC after consulting the developers who will build the solution, and record the outcome in an architecture decision record.
2. **Peer leadership.** I lead as a peer, support engineers in doing their best work, and keep work progressing. I change my position when a different opinion rests on a real problem that the other person has understood.
3. **People development.** Delegation is part of planning. The development of each engineer follows two inputs: the engineer's own goals, and what the current requirements need.
4. **Shared product.** A client requirement first passes a business analysis of its relevance. A relevant requirement is generalised into a product feature. A requirement that cannot be generalised is funded by the client and released behind a feature flag for that client only.
5. **Simplicity.** Every service, database and pipeline adds operating cost and cognitive load. I treat consolidation as a deliverable and plan it like any other feature.
6. **Documentation.** Architecture notes and agent instructions live in each repository and, where needed, in each module. Engineers and agents see what a change affects before they make it, which keeps changes targeted.
7. **Measured outcomes.** I judge a change by its measured effect on cost, lead time and release cadence, compared before and after the change.

### 4.2 Content rules

- Clients are neither named nor described individually. Employers may be named.
- Contact data is limited to LinkedIn and GitHub. No email address, phone number, postal address, date of birth, photograph or nationality. The professional facts in 4.1 are permitted.
- No metrics, headcounts or percentages. Scope and outcomes are described qualitatively. Periods and years in 4.1 are permitted.
- Language: English. Formal register, no contractions, no idioms, no emojis.
- Variants may write their own headlines and connective copy, but every factual statement must trace to sections 3 and 4.
